A woman in her early thirties with a warm, slightly husky alto voice and a faint British inflection. This is a pristine, high-quality studio recording with no background noise.

She takes a slow breath, steadying herself.

"I kept telling myself it didn't matter," she begins quietly, her voice low and controlled. "That I could just... walk away from it all."

A pause. Her fingers tap against something — a table, perhaps.

"But you don't walk away from something like that, do you?" A bitter half-laugh escapes her. "You carry it. Every single day."

Her voice tightens, climbing slightly in pitch.

"Und dann stehst du da — mitten in der Nacht — und fragst dich, ob es jemals anders hätte sein können."

She exhales slowly, a trembling breath that speaks of exhaustion more than sadness.

"Maybe it couldn't. Maybe that's the answer I've been avoiding."

The words hang in the air. She doesn't add anything. The silence says enough.

Let the final beat sit. No rush to resolve. The audience should feel the weight of what she's chosen not to say.
